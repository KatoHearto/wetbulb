wetbulb

Heat kills more people than any other kind of weather — and the number in every warning is the wrong one.

Air temperature says almost nothing about whether a body can shed heat. Wet-bulb temperature decides it. At 45 °C in dry desert air a healthy person sweats and survives. At 35 °C in humid air they cannot, because there is nowhere for the sweat to go.

what this is

A calculator and a set of decisions, not a warning service. Give it two numbers, or let it fetch real hourly weather for a place — the sliders keep working either way.

No account, no tracking. Works with no network at all — and if you ask it for real weather, it sends only your coordinates to Open-Meteo.

    What this cannot do

    not a doctor

    Nothing here is medical advice, and the thresholds are calibrated to published physiology, not to you. If someone is confused, agitated, or has stopped sweating in heat, that is an emergency — call, do not calculate.

    not a forecast

    It does not know what the weather is unless you ask it to look. Official heat warnings know things this does not, including how long the heat has already lasted.

    two limits, not one

    35 °C wet bulb is the theoretical limit. Around 31 °C is where strain has actually been measured on young healthy subjects. Quoting only the 35 makes the danger look four degrees further away than it is.

    shade, and standing still

    Every number here assumes shade and rest. Direct sun adds the equivalent of several degrees; physical work can multiply your heat production tenfold.
